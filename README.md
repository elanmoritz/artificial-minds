# Artificial Minds

Initially, surveys and a structured taxonomy of the "artificial mind"
literature — books, arXiv preprints, and cognitive architectures. This
will then grow to more detailed analyses and recommendations.

## Why this repository exists

The phrase "artificial mind" turns out to be a strange object of study in
its own right. It has attracted a handful of serious academic works across
three decades — Franklin's *Artificial Minds* (1995), Hoya's *Artificial
Mind System* (2005), Magnani's *Semiotic Brains and Artificial Minds*
(2007/09), Raikov's *Artificial Mind* (2024), Louwerse's *Understanding
Artificial Minds Through Human Minds* (2025) — a live population of GitHub
cognitive-architecture projects (OpenCog/Hyperon, LIDA, OpenNARS, Numenta
HTM), and a body of arXiv preprints that circle the same question under
different names ("mind design space," "psyche," "cognitive architecture").
Almost none of these works cite one another. This repository collects and
cross-references that literature, and will use it as the theoretical
foundation for further analysis and, eventually, concrete recommendations.

## What's here (and what's coming)

- **Surveys** of the book-length, arXiv, and GitHub literature on
  artificial minds and cognitive architectures.
- **A structured taxonomy** — a machine-readable comparison of the works
  surveyed, cross-tabulated by ontology of mind, architectural commitment,
  human/AI continuity, and treatment of phenomenality/consciousness.
- **Planned:** deeper analysis building on the surveys — identifying gaps
  in the current literature, and recommendations for both research and
  practical implementation.

This repository is also the theoretical home for
**[Athena Relenium](https://github.com/elanmoritz/relenium)**, an
in-development cognitive architecture project grounded explicitly in the
lineage surveyed here.

## Status

Early stage. Surveys are being reviewed in detail before publication here;
expect the structure above to fill in incrementally rather than all at
once.
